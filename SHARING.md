# Marginalia — trying it out

Thanks for taking a look! **Marginalia** is a study Bible for Mac and Windows that
recreates the classic Oxford wide-margin experience: beautiful reading, four bundled
translations, cross-references down the middle, and full "pencil marking" —
highlights, underlines, and notes — all saved on *your* computer and fully yours.

This is an early build shared for feedback, so expect a rough edge or two.

## Install

Download the build for your computer from the
[**Releases**](https://github.com/mrwboilers/marginalia/releases/latest) page.
Nothing in the app talks to the internet; it runs entirely offline on your machine.

### On a Mac

1. Open **`Marginalia_…_universal.dmg`** and drag **Marginalia** into **Applications**.
2. Open **Marginalia**. The first time, macOS shows a routine *"downloaded from the
   Internet — are you sure you want to open it?"* prompt; click **Open**. (The Mac
   build is signed and notarized by Apple, so there's no scary warning and no
   Terminal step.)

### On Windows

1. Download **`Marginalia_…_x64-setup.exe`** and run it.
2. If **"Windows protected your PC"** appears, click **More info → Run anyway** — it
   only shows because the Windows installer isn't signed yet.

## What to try

- **Read & navigate** — pick any book/chapter (top bar), or use the ‹ › arrows.
- **Switch translations** — the translation dropdown (top-left) swaps between four
  bundled, fully offline texts: the **King James Version (KJV)**, the **World English
  Bible (WEB)**, the **Berean Standard Bible (BSB)**, and **Young's Literal Translation
  (YLT)**. Try the same chapter in each — e.g. John 1 — to compare the wording. Your
  notes and bookmarks follow the verse across every translation; highlights and
  underlines are per-translation, since they attach to the exact words on the page.
- **Search** — full-text search across all 66 books of whichever translation you're
  reading.
- **Cross-references** — the center column. *Hover* a reference to preview the passage;
  *click* it to jump there.
- **Highlight / Underline** — pick the tool, then drag across text (or click a word).
- **Notes** — pick the **Note** tool, click a verse, and write a **rich-text** note:
  bold, headings, lists, links, even pasted/dropped **images**. Notes live in the margin
  next to their verse; hover to read, click to edit.
- **Strong's numbers** — on the **KJV**, toggle to see the original Hebrew/Greek word
  behind each word (hover a number for the definition). The toggle is hidden on the
  other translations, which aren't Strong's-tagged.
- **Bible Companion** — the "Companion" button opens Robert Roberts' daily reading plan,
  on today's date. Check off portions as you read; browse other days.
- **Font size** and **Export / Import** your markings are in the top bar.

## Your notes are yours

Everything you mark is saved locally and automatically. Use **Export** to save a backup
file of all your markings — and if you hit a bug, sending me that file helps me
reproduce it.

## Known rough edges (no need to report these)

- There's no side-by-side compare view yet — you switch translations one at a time.
- The note editor is a panel in the margin; for a note near the **bottom** of a long
  chapter it can open partly off-screen — scroll up a little and it'll fit.
- It's an early build, so if something feels off, that's exactly what I want to hear.

## What I'd love feedback on

- Does the **reading** experience feel good — typography, layout, navigation?
- **Translations** — is switching between them clear? Which ones do you actually use,
  and is there a public-domain translation you'd want added?
- Is **note-taking** intuitive? Anything you wanted to do and couldn't?
- The **Bible Companion** — useful? Would you actually read along?
- Anything confusing, broken, or missing.

Just message me — and attach an Export file if it's about a specific bug. Thank you!
